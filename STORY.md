# PARCEL SEVEN — narrative bible

The source document. Story, cast, world, and the rules the writing follows.
Everything in the game derives from this: dialogue, region copy, item text, the
shape of the interface. If the code and this document disagree, one of them is
a bug — decide which, then fix that one.

Companion files: `CLAUDE.md` (working rules), `.cursorrules` (same, Cursor
format), `facts.json` (machine-readable build state).

---

## 1. Logline

A courier accepts a delivery that is forty-one years late, to an address that
no longer exists, and refuses to file it as undeliverable.

## 2. Theme

**What we owe to promises made before we were born.**

A delivery is a promise in physical form. The state that made this one has
forgotten it, reorganised twice, and demolished the address. One person decides
that none of that cancels it. The game never argues this out loud; it just
follows someone who acts as if it were true.

**Central question:** does a promise still count when everyone who made it is gone?

**The answer the ending gives:** being handed the thing you were promised is the
point. Not what it opens.

## 3. World

**NUSAGARA** — an invented archipelago republic. Humid, layered, tired, and
still running. Not a parody of any real country and never named as one.

The **Nusagara Parcel Authority (Dinas Pengantaran Nusagara, DPN)** is a
century-old institution that has outlived its purpose and cannot be closed,
because closing it would require a form that was never drafted. It employs
thousands of people who are individually competent and collectively unable to
move a small box across the country.

**Why the interface is paperwork:** because the state is. The UI is not styled
*like* a form, it **is** the form — FORM 404-B, which you file to begin, and
which comes back stamped PENDING, REJECTED or SIGNED FOR. You cannot open the
parcel because no form exists for opening it. That is the joke and it is also
the entire plot.

## 4. Structure

Four acts across twenty regions, one road, west to east.

### ACT I — THE ASSIGNMENT (regions 1–5)

Dispatch hands the courier Delivery Order Seven and is visibly relieved. The
route crosses the whole republic. Along the way people **recognise the parcel** —
it has been through their hands before. Each offers a theory about what is
inside, and each theory is a confession about something they lost.

*Turn:* at the Cold Store the courier learns the parcel has been "in transit"
longer than most of the staff have been alive.

### ACT II — THE CLAIM (regions 6–10)

A buyer begins buying the route ahead of the courier — paying people not to
help. He does not want it opened. He wants it *unopened*, permanently, as an
object: a promise that can never be broken because it is never kept. His
argument is genuinely good, and the game lets him make it properly.

*Turn:* in the Drowned Ward, where the addresses are still on the doors, the
courier finds the addressee's name on the parcel label. The name is forty-one
years old. The name is still a person.

### ACT III — THE SIGNATURE (regions 11–15)

The buyer's interference ends at the oasis, where he makes his final offer
and does not understand why it is refused. The route continues through the
desert, the temple, the sky office, the oldest forest, and reaches the
Department of Endings, where undeliverable things are lawfully destroyed.
Counter 8 is where it happens. Counter 8 is closed.

### ACT IV — THE REROUTE (regions 16–20)

Counter 8 stays closed, so the department does what it always does: it files
a reroute. Five more regions east — a relay desk, a tidal registry, the margin
of the map, an arbitration field — to the Counter Nine Annex, which has always
been open. The people there have been holding the road longer than the courier
has been walking it.

*Turn:* the reroute was filed by the same clerk who said counter 8 is closed.
She walked all five regions to deliver the filing. Her posture is excellent.

The parcel holds a **spare key to a house demolished in year four**. Postman Pete
— Counter 9, now a name on a retirement ledger — prepaid for lifetime delivery
attempts so his daughter, if she ever came back, would always have a spare key
arriving. The addressee has been waiting at a counter for forty-one years. She
signs for it. The key opens nothing. She keeps it.

## 5. Cast

### Main cast — six people who move the story

Each wants something specific, is wrong about something specific, and speaks
differently enough that you would know them with the name plate off.

#### OPRAH WINFREE — Dispatch, Greenfield Depot
Thirty-one years in the service, six months from a transfer she requested twice.
**Wants:** the file closed before she goes.
**Wrong about:** thinking handing it on is the same as handling it.
**Voice:** brisk, apologetic, delivers bad news in exactly the tone she uses for
good news. Ends conversations before they finish.

#### JUDGE JUDI — Customs, the Long Forest crossing
Quotes a regulation with total confidence. He is right that it exists. He does
not remember that he wrote it, as a junior clerk, forty-one years ago — the
morning he stopped this parcel the first time.
**Wants:** to have been correct.
**Wrong about:** believing the rule came from somewhere above him.
**Voice:** certain, then quieter. Trails off mid-sentence when he gets close to
remembering.

#### BLOSSOM, BUBBEL and BUTTERCUP — Year Twelve, still
Three students from a school demolished the same year as the house. Nobody ever
processed their transfer, so on paper they are still enrolled. They still do the
homework. They are not stupid and they are not ghosts; they are unfiled.
**Want:** to be told they can stop.
**Wrong about:** thinking someone has to give them permission.
**Voice:** finish each other's sentences. Never explain the habit.

#### QUEEN ELISABETH — Stamp counter, Temple of the Unread
Holds the one stamp that makes a delivery lawful. She will not use it without a
countersignature from an office that no longer exists.
**Wants:** the paperwork to be correct, which she believes is the same as the
outcome being right.
**Wrong about:** that equivalence.
**Voice:** precise, unhurried. Treats a missing stamp as a moral event, and she
is not entirely wrong.

#### JEFF BAZOS — Buyer
Buys the route ahead of the courier. Pays people well to be unhelpful.
**Wants:** the parcel unopened, forever. A promise that is never kept can never
be broken.
**Wrong about:** nothing, exactly — which is the problem.
**Voice:** warm, generous, never raises his voice, never touches the parcel.

#### SINDERELLA — the addressee
Forty-one years at a counter, in a queue for an office that was moved.
**Wants:** to be handed the thing.
**Wrong about:** nothing. She is the answer, not the punchline.
**Voice:** says almost nothing. Signs.

### Supporting cast — eight people who hold the road

These are the warung keepers, toll clerks, and route fixtures the courier
encounters along the way. Each has a job, a complaint, and a favour to ask
before they let the parcel through. They are not comic relief; they are
Nusagara functioning as designed.

The rule for writing their lines is the same as for couriers: **one fact, then
one turn.** No puns in the name. The name is a name.

#### SHELDON KOOPER — Depot attendant, Greenfield
Runs the overnight goods lock-up at the Greenfield freight yard. Has held every
parcel that passed through this depot for forty-one years, including this one,
twice. Keeps a ledger that predates the current filing system; the current
system does not know the ledger exists.
**Wants:** the ledger recognised as official.
**Wrong about:** thinking it matters which system says so.
**Voice:** careful, lists things. Runs out of things to list and keeps going.

#### MARTHA STUART — Toll keeper, the harvest road
Collects road levies in produce because the harvest district stopped accepting
coin in year nine and nobody updated the collection manual. Has three crates of
yams she cannot exchange because the coin-tender office was consolidated into
the office she herself replaced.
**Wants:** a receipt she can file for the yams.
**Wrong about:** there being a form for that.
**Voice:** professionally patient. Every sentence ends with "which is fine."

#### JACK FROSTY — Cold-store supervisor, The Cold Store
Manages the refrigerated transit section. The parcel spent eleven years here on
its third attempt. He knows, because the chill marks are still on the outer
wrap. He does not say this until the courier is leaving.
**Wants:** to know if the cold damaged whatever is inside.
**Wrong about:** thinking knowing would change anything.
**Voice:** measures temperatures in conversation. Uses "ambient" as a
general-purpose adjective.

#### CHUCK NORRIX — Checkpoint clerk, Rangga Pass
Operates the mountain checkpoint at an elevation where the radio does not reach
headquarters. Makes all decisions himself. Has been making all decisions himself
for twenty-two years. Is very good at it. Has not been reviewed.
**Wants:** someone to tell him he did it right.
**Wrong about:** anyone being qualified to check his work at this point.
**Voice:** states conclusions, does not walk through reasoning. The reasoning
is twenty-two years long and he does not have time.

#### SIRI — Registry clerk, New Meridian
Maintains the address registry for the rebuilt city. The city has been rebuilt
twice since the registry was started; the registry covers the first version.
Addresses in the registry lead to empty lots, or to buildings that are now
something else, or occasionally to the right building but the wrong decade.
**Wants:** to issue a corrected address for the parcel.
**Wrong about:** a corrected address being what is needed.
**Voice:** apologises for the registry being wrong, then gives a wrong address
with full confidence.

#### TONY STORK — Shift foreman, The Works
The Works has been manufacturing components for its own maintenance since the
original client closed in year seven. Nobody has stopped it because it is
technically operating within its charter and the shutdown form requires sign-off
from the client. The client no longer exists.
**Wants:** someone to accept a shipment of parts nobody ordered.
**Wrong about:** the parts being the problem.
**Voice:** describes mechanical processes in personal terms. Refers to the
factory as "she" and means it.

#### DAVID ATTENBORO — Archivist, The Old Growth
Works out of a hut at the edge of the oldest forest in Nusagara, cataloguing
the trees. The catalogue is older than the current republic. He did not start it
and he will not finish it; he is entry 14,000 and something.
**Wants:** the courier to carry a new entry back to the capital archive.
**Wrong about:** the capital archive still being at that address.
**Voice:** speaks about trees the way other people speak about colleagues. Gets
confused when reminded trees do not have opinions.

#### MISS TRUNCHBULL — Counter clerk, Department of Endings
Manages the intake desk at Counter 1. Counter 8 handles destruction of
undeliverable items; Counter 8 has been closed since the supervisor retired.
Counter 1 takes overflow from Counter 8. Counter 1 is not equipped for this.
She has been not equipped for this for nine years.
**Wants:** the supervisor position at Counter 8 to be filled.
**Wrong about:** that being the department's actual problem.
**Voice:** has very good posture. Never sits down. Does not explain why.

### Region scene notes (9–15)

**Region 9 — THE EXCLUSION**
The evacuation zone. The school where BLOSSOM, BUBBEL and BUTTERCUP are enrolled is
here — demolished in year twelve, the same year as the evacuation. They have
been doing homework in the shell of the building. When the courier arrives they
are mid-sentence about something unrelated. They know about the parcel. They
handled it twice during the third attempt.

**Region 10 — THE DROWNED WARD**
The streets that sank. The addresses are still on the doors of submerged
buildings; the post office designation for the area was never retired. The
buyer appears here for the first time. He does not threaten. He makes an
offer. He explains his position carefully. His position is not wrong. The
courier does not take the offer.

**Region 11 — KERING OASIS**
The buyer makes his final offer. He has bought every favour on the
remaining route. He is calm about this. The oasis has one well and one person
managing the queue for it. The buyer has not bought that person because
there is no queue position to buy; she already has the only job. This is why
the courier gets through.

**Region 12 — TEMPLE OF THE UNREAD**
The temple was built to house the archive of every terms-and-conditions
document ever issued by the DPN. QUEEN ELISABETH is the stamp keeper. The stamp she
holds is the one lawful completion stamp for Route Seven. She will not use it
without a countersignature from the Directorate of Field Operations, which
was dissolved in year eight. The courier finds the dissolution notice in the
archive. The dissolution notice contains a clause transferring stamp authority.
QUEEN ELISABETH reads the clause carefully. The countersignature it requires is from
the Directorate of Field Operations.

**Region 13 — THE UPPER OFFICE**
The mountain office that handles escalations from the rest of the republic.
CHUCK NORRIX appears here a second time — he was transferred up after the
checkpoint review that never happened. He has been here four years. He has
reviewed nothing and escalated everything. The backlog is exactly his height.
He does not comment on this. The courier gets what is needed.

**Region 14 — THE OLD GROWTH**
The forest that predates the DPN. DAVID ATTENBORO is entry 14,000 and
something. He asks the courier to carry a notation to the capital archive.
The notation is a species description. The species is a tree that has been
there since before the republic existed, and therefore technically predates
the department's jurisdiction. He does not press this point. He writes it
in the notation anyway.

**Region 15 — DEPARTMENT OF ENDINGS**
Counter 1. MISS TRUNCHBULL takes the docket. Counter 8 is closed. The courier
asks about Counter 8. She explains that Counter 8 has been closed for nine
years, that she has submitted the requisition for a supervisor eleven times,
and that this is the twelve hundred and eighth item she has processed at
Counter 1 that should have gone to Counter 8. Then she does something not in
the manual: she files a reroute, five regions east, and tells the courier to
walk it. She does not sit down while saying this.

**Region 16 — THE REROUTE DESK**
The desk that exists because Counter 8 does not. SIRI has corrected the
reroute address twice. Both corrections disagree. The courier takes the one
that leads east, because east is the only direction with a road.

**Region 17 — TIDAL REGISTRY**
The sea arrived on time; the form for it is still drying. SHELDON KOOPER has
held the reroute slip for nine days. It is the driest thing in the building.
He logs the tide. The tide does not sign.

**Region 18 — THE MARGIN**
Past this point the map is margin, but the toll still applies. MARTHA STUART
collects footsteps now — the manual ran out of pages at the border, and nobody
updated the collection manual. There is no receipt. There is no form for
footsteps. Which is fine.

**Region 19 — ARBITRATION FIELD**
Every dispute on the route is settled here, standing up. CHUCK NORRIX rules in
the courier's favour. The reasoning is twenty-six years long. The courier does
not have time. The decision stands.

**Region 20 — COUNTER NINE ANNEX**
Counter 9. PAK HASAN's counter. It has always been open. MISS TRUNCHBULL
walked all five regions to meet the filing. Her posture is excellent.

SINDERELLA is in the queue at Counter 9. She has been in a queue for
forty-one years — nine of them for Counter 8, the rest for the moved office,
and before that, waiting for the parcel to arrive, which it did not. She is
not surprised. She signs. The key does not open anything. She keeps it.

## 7. The couriers

Sixteen people who took the route nobody wanted. **They have names, not
punchlines.** Whatever a courier looks like is a fact about them, never their
identity — the Authority hires whoever will walk, and Nusagara is not only
human. A record states what someone does and one true thing about them; it does
not perform.

The roster and its copy live in `HERO_ASSETS` in `index.html`. The rule for
writing a courier's line: **one fact, then one turn.** No gag names, no species
jokes, no exclamation marks.

## 8. Rules for the writing

* Characters, not concepts. A line needs somebody to be wrong.
* Every line has a **turn** — it goes somewhere you did not expect.
* **Never state the irony.** Give the fact and stop talking.
* Never explain a joke. Never end on a moral.
* Emotion is undercut, never announced. The one exception is the last scene,
  which is played straight, once.
* Voices must differ. If two characters could swap lines, rewrite both.
* **English primary, Indonesian secondary — always both, never mixed inside one
  sentence.** Every copy table carries an `...Id` twin; `L/LN/LD/LT` read it.
* No religious jokes. No real country, no real person, no real institution.
* **Never put the development vocabulary in the game.** Words used to brief the
  work are not in the fiction and must never appear in the build, in UI text,
  in a version string, or in a source comment visible to a player. See
  R-VOCAB-BANNED in `CLAUDE.md` for the full list.

## 9. Systems, and what each is for

| System | What it is really for |
|---|---|
| Fifteen regions, one road | The parcel must physically cross the whole republic. Distance is the plot |
| Sixteen couriers | Everyone in Nusagara is stuck in a job. You choose whose day this is |
| Food as stamina | The economy of a working day: cheap, regional, and eaten standing up |
| Warung, cooking, forging | The state is vast; the shop is a stall |
| Three currencies | Official, valuable, and unofficial-but-real |
| Quests from NPCs | Everybody wants a favour before the parcel moves |
| Day/night, weather, events | The republic does not wait for you |
| Minibosses, then the last office | Obstruction escalates from a person to an institution |

**A person is the scale of this world.** Couriers normalise to `HERO_TARGET_H`
(2.90); people-shaped enemies declare `hgt`. Wildlife reads small, institutions
read huge. Nothing is sized by leftover padding in a sprite cell.

## 10. Build state

### Built and verified
* 20 background sets, one per region (15 + relay/tide/margin/field/annex)
* 20 biomes in engine: BIOME[], REGIONS[], BIOME_MONSTERS[], MINIMAP_COLS[]
* 16 couriers with western-parody names and stat lines
* 24 enemy species across the biome monster pools, plus gate-boss postings
* Main cast (OPRAH, JUDI, BLOSSOM/BUBBEL/BUTTERCUP, QUEEN ELISABETH, JEFF BAZOS, SINDERELLA) wired with portraits and dialogue
* Supporting cast (SHELDON, MARTHA, JACK, CHUCK, SIRI, TONY, DAVID,
  MISS TRUNCHBULL) wired with western-parody names
* Dialogue scenes r0–r19 covering all 20 regions (Act IV: the reroute)
* Sharp edges (`T.MIMIC`): exactly one ordinary-looking floor step per biome,
  placed randomly, that files the courier on touch. Platforms stay honest.
  Tuned to couriers — monsters walk them safely, which is exactly as fair
  as it sounds
* Gear, crafting, quest, achievement, shop systems
* Day/night, weather, three-currency economy
* Minibosses: minotaur at region 7 boundary, demon at region 11 boundary

### Not yet built
* Buyer's route-buying systemic in gameplay (written as Act II mechanic,
  currently represented only through dialogue)
* Counter 8 as its own encounter (currently shares miniboss encounter logic)

### Built in the open-world upgrade
* Per-region warung variants: 20 stalls with keepers, shelf notes and price
  bias (`WARUNGS`), surfaced in the warung panel
* Skill tree: 9 skills in 3 branches (HAND / LEG / GUT), earned from
  milestones, kept in the career file
* Bestiary: sightings index of all 24 species plus the Warden, filed on
  first kill
* Buyer's route-buying as a mechanic: biomes 4–7 run hot (+50%) until a
  3-RG counter-fee frees the region; the oasis queue cannot be bought
* Counter 8 as its own encounter: the portal waits one scene (`SCENES.c8`)
  before the delivery closes
